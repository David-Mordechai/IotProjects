Wire the CC1101 radio to your ESP32

Both boards sit right side up with their labels on top. Their pins point down, so the wire plugs go on from underneath. Press Plugs underneath above the model, or drag it around, to see them from below. Build this twice: one board sends, the other receives. Both are wired the same way.

Steps

    Connections

    WireESP32 pinCC1101 pin
    1 GND2 VCC 3 GDO04 CSN 5 SCK6 MOSI 7 MISO8 GDO2

    CC1101 header seen from above, with the antenna on the right. The board has "1 2" printed above the header and "7 8" below it. You need 8 female-to-female wires per board, 16 in total. Wire color doesn't matter electrically.

    Upload and test

    The code is in firmware/radio-hello. Plug in one board at a time and upload:

    # board A, the sender
pio run -e sender -t upload
# board B, the receiver
pio run -e receiver -t upload
# watch what board B hears
pio device monitor

    On the receiver you should see one line per second:

    got: hello #12      RSSI  -41.5 dBm  LQI 3

    RSSI is signal strength: around −30 dBm is very strong, −100 dBm is barely heard. If the monitor says the CC1101 was not found, unplug USB and recheck the wires, especially MOSI and MISO.

    How accurate is this model?

    Matches your hardware
    • ESP32 pin order and names, from your photo of the HW-394.
    • CC1101 header numbering (1 2 at the top, 7 8 at the bottom), and the 433M and V2.0 markings, from your photo.
    • Pin spacing of 2.54 mm on both boards.
    Simplified
    • The CC1101 pin names (GND, VCC, SCK...) are not printed on your board. They come from the standard pinout for this board design.
    • The CC1101 board size (about 30 × 14.5 mm) and part positions are estimated from the photo.
    • Both boards' pins point down, so the wire plugs go on from underneath, and the boards rest on those plugs. Use the Plugs underneath view to see them.
    • Wires are drawn neatly. Real 10 cm wires loop and drape. The radio waves are an illustration.
