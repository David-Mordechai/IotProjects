    Outside node wiring

    WireESP32Part pin

    The node needs 3 grounds and 2 parts on 3V3, but the HW-394 has only one 3V3 pin and two GND pins. A small breadboard's power rails share them. Needs the breadboard (not owned yet).

    Home station wiring

    WireESP32Part pin

    The buzzer's BB- pin sits at 5 V when off, so it goes through an NPN transistor, never straight to a GPIO. Needs a breadboard, an NPN transistor and a 1 kΩ resistor. Exact holes, step by step: home-breadboard-3d.html.

    How accurate is this model?

    Matches the plan
    • ESP32 pin names and order, the CC1101 pinout (D4, D5, D18, D19, D22, D23) and the HC-SR04 pins (D25, D26), as in the wiring guides.
    • Radar OUT on D33, not D21: deep-sleep wake (ext0) works only on RTC pins (GPIO 0, 2, 4, 12–15, 25–27, 32–39).
    • WS2812 data on D13 and its power from VIN (5 V); buzzer driven through a transistor on D12.
    • Distance math: echo time ÷ 58 = cm.
    Simplified
    • Not to scale: the visitor is a small token, and 1 cm of distance is drawn as 0.45 mm.
    • Wires are drawn from the top of each pin and arc over; real jumpers plug in under the board.
    • Part shapes are approximate blocks; the 3D wiring guides have the exact layouts.
    • Timing is slowed down. A real cycle takes well under a second.
    • Deep sleep on the HW-394 still draws several mA (its regulator and USB chip stay on), so a power bank lasts days, not months. Real battery life needs a bare low-power board later.
